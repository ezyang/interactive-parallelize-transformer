Tensor Parallelism

Syntax:

In[B, DY] ·D Win[D, FY] ·F Wout[FY, D] → Out[B, DY]

(we use Y to eventually combine with FSDP)

In a fully-sharded data-parallel AllReduce we move the weights across chips. We can also shard the feedforward dimension of the model and move the activations during the layer — this is called “1D model parallelism” or Megatron sharding (Shoeybi et al. 2019). This can unlock a smaller efficient batch size per pod.adaptation This is the flat orange line from the roofline primer: when the activations move, the comms cost scales with the batch just as the compute does, so its efficiency doesn’t hinge on the per-chip batch at all — which is exactly why it can unlock a smaller efficient batch per pod. As noted, In[B, DY] ·D Win[D, FY] ·F Wout[FY, D] → Out[B, DY] means we have to gather our activations before the first matmul. Here's the algorithm for tensor parallelism!

Tensor Parallelism:adaptation Watch the phrase on critical path. With pure data parallelism the AllReduce happened after the loss was already computed, so the network could grind away while the chips moved on. Here the matmuls cannot start until the gathers finish — these collectives sit squarely in the layer’s serial path.

Forward pass: need to compute Loss[B]

  1. In[B, D] = AllGather(In[B, DY]) (on critical path)
  2. Tmp[B, FY] = In[B, D] ·D Win[D, FY] (not sharded along contracting, so no comms)
  3. Out[B, D] {UY} = Tmp[B, FY] ·F Wout[FY, D]
  4. Out[B, DY] = ReduceScatter(Out[B, D] {UY}) (on critical path)
  5. Loss[B] = …

Backward pass: need to compute dWout[FY, D], dWin[D, FY]

  1. dOut[B, DY] = …
  2. dOut[B, D] = AllGather(dOut[B, DY]) (on critical path)
  3. dWout[FY, D] = Tmp[B, FY] ·B dOut[B, D]
  4. dTmp[B, FY] = dOut[B, D] ·D Wout[FY, D] (can throw away dOut[B, D] here)
  5. In[B, D] = AllGather(In[B, DY]) (this can be skipped by sharing with (1) from the forward pass)
  6. dWin[D, FY] = In[B, D] ·B dTmp[B, FY]
  7. dIn[B, D] {UY} = dTmp[B, FY] ·F Win[D, FY] (needed for previous layers)
  8. dIn[B, DY] = ReduceScatter(dIn[B, D] {UY}) (on critical path)

One nice thing about tensor parallelism is that it interacts nicely with the two matrices in our Transformer forward pass. Naively, we would do an AllReduce after each of the two matrices. But here we first do In[B, DY] · Win[D, FY] → Tmp[B, FY] and then Tmp[B, FY] · Wout[FY, D] → Out[B, DY]. This means we AllGather In at the beginning, and ReduceScatter Out at the end, rather than doing an AllReduce.adaptation And since an AllReduce is itself an AllGather plus a ReduceScatter, one AllGather in and one ReduceScatter out is half the bytes of the naive two-AllReduce plan.

How costly is this? Let's only model the forward pass - the backwards pass is just the transpose of each operation here. In 1D tensor parallelism we AllGather the activations before the first matmul, and ReduceScatter them after the second, sending two bytes at a time (bf16).
