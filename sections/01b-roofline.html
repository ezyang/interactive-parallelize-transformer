Everything in this chapter — every scheme, every derivation, every takeaway — is one picture wearing four costumes. Before we meet the costumes, let's get the picture into your fingers.

When a chip works on one layer of our Transformer, two clocks run at the same time:

The compute clock: the MXU has to chew through this layer's share of FLOPs. With B = tokens split over DP = chips, that's 4 · B · D · k·FDP · C = per layer (k·F because a token only multiplies through its k activated experts).

The network clock: whatever bytes this scheme moves have to squeeze through the interconnect at Wici = . Crucially, the hardware lets these two clocks overlap: the network can carry bytes while the MXU multiplies. So a layer doesn't cost the sum of the two times — it costs the max. Communication that fits under the compute clock is free. Communication that pokes out past it leaves silicon idle, and that overhang is exactly what "communication-bound" means.

Try: with the toggle on weights, drag B = down and watch only the compute bar shrink — the network bar doesn't hear the batch size at all, so at some point the comms must poke out. Then flip to activations and drag again: now both bars move together, and no amount of batch will save you.

That toggle is the deepest fact in this chapter. Every parallelism scheme is a choice about what travels:

  • If what travels is weights — 2·D·E·F bytes (every one of the E experts, active or not), as in data parallelism and FSDP — the network clock is fixed, while the compute clock scales with your per-chip batch. Big batch ⇒ compute wins ⇒ comms hidden.
  • If what travels is activations — 2·B·D bytes, as in tensor parallelism — batch size appears on both clocks and cancels. Hiding then depends on the model's shape (the width a token multiplies through: k·F) instead.

Now the roofline itself. In Part 1 of the original book, a single chip was compute-bound only when its arithmetic intensity — FLOPs per byte touched — beat the ratio of FLOP speed to memory bandwidth. The identical logic applies here, one level up, with the interconnect playing the role of memory. For weight-moving schemes, your FLOPs scale with B/DP and your bytes don't, so:
